Date:   2026-10-06

Author: HOEOS certification process
Predecessor: G5-M01 (REJECTED - partial fix, 4/8 regression pass)
Revision: 1 - resolves x.5 contradiction in Capability 1

--------------------------------------------------------------------
1. MOTIVATION
--------------------------------------------------------------------

G5-M01 attempted to repair sentence segmentation with a single
regex change to documentNormalizer.ts:134:

  Before: /[^.!?]+[.!?]+(?:\s+|$)|[^.!?]+$/g
  After:  /.+?(?:[.!?](?=\s|$)|$)/gs

Result: 4/8 regression pass. The candidate handled numeric periods
(10.1038, 99.4%) but not abbreviation periods (Dr., Prof., Vol.).
The lookahead (?=\s|$) still matches when an abbreviation period
is followed by a space. A regex cannot distinguish "abbreviation
followed by space" from "sentence terminator followed by space"
without external context.

Conclusion: G5-M01 rejected. A single-regex approach cannot
satisfy the contract. G5-M02 must use a two-capability design.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
2. CAPABILITIES REQUIRED
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Capability 1 - NUMERIC PERIOD PROTECTION
  A period character is NOT a sentence terminator when:
    (a) the character immediately before it is a digit
    AND
    (b) the character immediately after it is a digit
        OR the character immediately after it is '%'
  Examples that MUST be preserved as lexical content:
    10.1038
    99.4%
    3.14
  Note: constructs of the form x.5 (letter-period-digit) are
  NOT protected by this capability. They are out of scope for
  G5-M02. If a future migration needs them, they will be added
  as G5-M03 members with their own acceptance cases.

Capability 2 - PROTECTED ABBREVIATION PROTECTION
  A period character is NOT a sentence terminator when the
  token immediately before it (delimited by whitespace or
  start-of-string) matches a member of the
  PROTECTED_ABBREVIATIONS_V1 set, and the character immediately
  after the period is whitespace or end-of-string.
  The set is defined in Section 4.

Capability 3 - SENTENCE TERMINATOR DETECTION
  A period, exclamation mark, or question mark IS a sentence
  terminator when:
    (a) neither Capability 1 nor Capability 2 applies
    AND
    (b) the terminator is followed by whitespace
        OR end-of-string
        OR the end of a paragraph

--------------------------------------------------------------------
3. ARCHITECTURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Selected approach: TWO-PASS MASKING

  Pass 1 - MASK
    Replace every protected period (as defined by Capabilities
    1 and 2) with the sentinel character U+E000.

  Pass 2 - SEGMENT
    Apply the existing sentence segmentation regex to the masked
    text. The regex remains:
      /[^.!?]+[.!?]+(?:\s+|$)|[^.!?]+$/g
    except that periods followed by U+E000 do not match as
    terminators, because U+E000 is not in [.!?].

  Pass 3 - UNMASK
    Restore every U+E000 in each segment's text back to '.'.

  Result: the segmenter sees a text where protected periods are
  invisible to terminator detection. Sentence boundaries fall
  only at genuine terminators.

Sentinel choice:
  U+E000 (Private Use Area, BMP). Not producible by any normal
  text input. Must be validated as absent from the raw document
  before masking; if present, ABORT (do not silently corrupt).

Abort behavior on sentinel collision:
  If U+E000 is present in the raw document, maskProtectedPeriods
  throws an Error. The caller (segmentDocument) does not catch
  it. The segmentation call fails loudly rather than silently
  proceeding with unprotected boundaries.

  Rationale: silent fallback hides a document-level integrity
  failure. A document containing the Private Use Area sentinel
  is not a normal input and should be rejected rather than
  processed with degraded segmentation.

Out of scope:
  canonicalTextOffsetService.ts:127 uses a different regex
  /[^.!?]+[.!?]*/g and produces 10 matches on SAMPLE_TEXT_A.
  Unifying the two segmenters is a separate migration G5-M03.
  G5-M02 touches documentNormalizer.ts ONLY.

--------------------------------------------------------------------
4. PROTECTED ABBREVIATIONS V1
--------------------------------------------------------------------

The following tokens, when immediately followed by a period and
then whitespace or end-of-string, are protected. Case-insensitive.

  Dr
  Prof
  Mr
  Mrs
  Ms
  Vol
  No
  pp
  ed
  eds
  cf
  al
  vs
  i.e
  e.g
  et al
  etc
  Fig
  Eqs
  Eq
  Sec
  Secs
  Ch
  Chap
  Pt
  Inc
  Ltd
  Co
  Corp
  St
  Jr
  Sr

Multi-token entries ("et al") are matched against the two tokens
immediately preceding the period, joined by a single space.

The set is declared as PROTECTED_ABBREVIATIONS_V1. Adding or
removing members is a separate migration.

5. ACCEPTANCE MATRIX
--------------------------------------------------------------------

Legacy cases (must all pass; unchanged from G5-M01):

  L1. preserves DOI decimal punctuation
       "The DOI is 10.1038/s41586-023-00001-x." -> 1 sentence
  L2. preserves percentage decimal punctuation
       "The model achieves 99.4% accuracy." -> 1 sentence
  L3. does not treat Dr. as a sentence boundary
       "By Dr. Aris Thorne. He published." -> 2 sentences
  L4. does not treat Prof. as a sentence boundary
       "By Prof. Elena Rostova. She published." -> 2 sentences
  L5. does not treat Vol. as a sentence boundary
       "Published in Vol. 14. The journal is authoritative."
       -> 2 sentences
  L6. recognizes normal sentence endings
       "This is one. This is two." -> 2 sentences
  L7. preserves all canonical tokens in the forensic fixture
       SAMPLE_TEXT_A -> sentence-token sum == 91 == canonical
  L8. preserves decimal and DOI regions as lexical content
       "Journal of Science, Vol. 14, DOI: 10.1038/..." -> 1 sentence

Expanded cases (new in G5-M02):

  E1.  multiple punctuation
       "Wait!!! Are you serious?" -> 2 sentences
  E2.  question mark terminator
       "Is this a question?" -> 1 sentence
  E3.  exclamation terminator
       "This is an exclamation!" -> 1 sentence
  E4.  newline boundary
       "First sentence.\nSecond sentence." -> 2 sentences
  E5.  paragraph boundary
       "First paragraph.\n\nSecond paragraph." -> 2 sentences
  E6.  Dr. at start, continuation
       "Dr. Aris Thorne published the paper." -> 1 sentence
  E7.  mixed Dr., Prof., Vol. sequence
       "Dr. Thorne, Prof. Rostova, Vol. 14, all agree."
       -> 1 sentence
  E8.  decimal with unit suffix
       "The result was 99.4% accuracy." -> 1 sentence
  E9.  DOI with trailing period at end
       "See DOI: 10.1038/s41586-023-00001-x." -> 1 sentence
  E10. abbreviation at end-of-document
       "Published by Prof." -> 1 sentence (no trailing space)

All 8 legacy + 10 expanded = 18 cases must pass 100%.
